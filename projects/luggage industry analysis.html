Abstract & Motivation

This project presents a programmatic framework for automated data retrieval, portfolio optimization, and empirical testing of two pillars of modern finance: the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) and the Weak-Form Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH). Using the yfinance API, a five-year daily historical dataset (January 1, 2021 – January 1, 2026) was constructed across 11 prominent NSE-listed Indian brands in the luggage, textile, and apparel retail sectors, benchmarked against the Nifty 50 Index (^NSEI).

Rather than testing an overly diversified broad index, concentrating on Indian consumer discretionary equities creates a sharper empirical sandbox: the sector is shaped by a rising middle class, a structural shift from unbranded to branded goods, and cyclical raw-material and supply-chain pressures—making it an ideal environment to test whether standard pricing models capture sector risk and whether localized momentum breaks market efficiency.

Methodology & Four-Tiered Workflow

  • Phase 1 — Automated Data Pipeline: Programmatically pulled Daily Adjusted Closing Prices via yfinance for 11 NSE tickers (VIPIND.NS, SAFARI.NS, TRENT.NS, ABFRL.NS, SHOPERSTOP.NS, PAGEIND.NS, KKCL.NS, MANYAVAR.NS, GOCOLORS.NS, LUXIND.NS, ARVIND.NS) plus ^NSEI. Adjusted close prices isolate true returns from stock splits, bonuses, and dividends. Minor trading-day misalignments across 1,235 trading days were resolved with forward-fill (ffill()) and backward-fill (bfill()).
  • Phase 2 — Portfolio Matrix Engineering: Converted daily prices into continuous log returns (R_t = ln(P_t / P_{t-1})) for time-additivity and constructed an equal-weighted portfolio (w_i = 1/11 ≈ 9.09%). Annualized expected return (E(R_p) = w^T R) and portfolio volatility (σ_p = √(w^T Σ w)) were scaled using a 252-trading-day year, yielding a 5.82% expected annual return and 20.02% annualized volatility.
  • Phase 3 — Auditing CAPM via OLS Regression: Regressed daily portfolio excess returns against Nifty 50 excess returns using an annualized risk-free rate of 6.50% (RBI 91-day Treasury Bill baseline, converted daily via 0.065 / 252) to estimate systematic Beta (β) and abnormal Alpha (α).
  • Phase 4 — Testing Weak-Form EMH: Evaluated whether historical returns follow an unpredictable random walk using the Ljung-Box Q-Test across lags 1, 2, 3, 5, and 10 alongside a 20-day Autocorrelation Function (ACF) plot against 95% confidence bounds.

Beta Assessment: A statistically significant market Beta of 0.8917 (t = 27.263, p < 0.001) shows the portfolio tracks the Nifty 50 closely while exhibiting roughly 11% lower systematic volatility than the broader market.

Alpha Assessment: The daily intercept of -0.0002 (p = 0.587) fails to reject H_0: α = 0, confirming zero statistically significant abnormal return and empirically validating CAPM's core pricing assumption.

Idiosyncratic Variance: An R² of 37.6% reveals that while market risk is highly significant, 62.4% of total return variance is driven by sector- and company-specific factors such as raw material costs, retail demand cycles, and corporate earnings.

Empirical Findings: Weak-Form EMH & Autocorrelation

Under the Weak-Form Efficient Market Hypothesis, current prices fully reflect past price history, meaning daily returns should behave as independent white noise with zero autocorrelation. Applying the Ljung-Box Q-Test across multiple lags yielded significant serial dependence:

Key Takeaways & Strategic Implications

CAPM Holds, But Leaves Sector Alpha Unexplained: Broad systematic beta accurately captures market co-movement, yet 62.4% idiosyncratic variance underscores that fundamental sector drivers—consumer sentiment, cotton prices, and brand-level execution—dominate individual outcomes.

The Consumption Momentum Anomaly: Rejecting Weak-Form EMH across Lags 1, 2, 3, and 10 demonstrates that prices in Indian consumer discretionary retail do not adjust instantaneously. Post-pandemic lifestyle shifts and institutional flows into premium leaders (such as Trent and Safari) create persistent short-term momentum.

Actionable Allocation Value: For systematic and quantitative strategies, significant short-horizon serial dependence suggests that momentum overlays or short-term multi-factor models may capture excess return within specialized Indian retail baskets.
