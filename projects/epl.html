EPL 2022–23: Evaluating Attacking Indices

Combining Python web scraping from FBref with interactive Tableau visualizations to evaluate English Premier League attackers beyond raw goals and assists—focusing on xG, xA, and ball progression.

View Scraping Code (Colab) → Open on Tableau Public →

Introduction

Traditional football metrics like goals scored and assists rarely tell the full story. Expected Goals (xG) and Expected Assists (xA) provide a deeper view by measuring the quality of chances taken and created, while Progressive Passes (PrP) and Progressive Carries (PrC) capture how effectively players advance the ball up the pitch and retain possession.

This project evaluates attacking players across the 2022–23 English Premier League season by pairing a Python web-scraping pipeline with a custom dark-themed Tableau dashboard.

Data Acquisition & Scraping Pipeline

Player statistics were scraped directly from FBref.com using Python and Pandas in Google Colab:

  • HTML Table Extraction: Used pd.read_html(url_df)[0] to parse the target FBref player statistics table directly into a DataFrame.
  • Multi-Level Header Cleanup: Flattened FBref's nested multi-index columns by stripping whitespace, parsing out level_0 prefixes to retain clean stat names, and resetting the DataFrame index.
  • Export for BI: Handled missing values and exported the cleaned dataset to premier_league_stats.csv for ingestion into Tableau.

Dashboard Design

Built on a high-contrast black canvas with dark silver chart panels, the dashboard brings together three core visual comparisons:

Goals vs. Expected Goals (Dumbbell Chart): Dual-axis comparison highlighting the gap between actual finishing output and underlying chance quality.

Assists vs. Expected Assists (Dumbbell Chart): Reveals which playmakers benefited from clinical teammates versus those whose created chances went unconverted.

Ball Progression & Carrying (Stacked Bar Chart): Ranks the top 10 players by combined progressive passing and progressive carrying to distinguish passers from press-resistant ball carriers.

Key Findings

Clinical Finishing Powered Man City's Title Charge: Erling Haaland (36.0 goals vs. 28.5 xG), Harry Kane (30.0 vs. 21.6 xG), Martin Ødegaard, and Gabriel Martinelli consistently outperformed their expected goals, converting half-chances into goals. By contrast, Mohamed Salah and Ollie Watkins fell short of their xG, leaving clear-cut opportunities on the table.

De Bruyne vs. Fernandes: Kevin De Bruyne posted 16 assists from 13.7 xA, amplified by Haaland's finishing. Meanwhile, Bruno Fernandes led the league in xA (16.8) yet finished with only 8 assists—a stark indicator of Manchester United's finishing deficit. Trent Alexander-Arnold showed a similar xA surplus at Liverpool.

Two Modes of Ball Progression: Ben White, Bruno Fernandes, and Martin Ødegaard drove progression primarily through incisive passing, whereas Pascal Groß, Rodri, and Kieran Trippier excelled at carrying the ball forward under pressure.
